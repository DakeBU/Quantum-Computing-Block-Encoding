# Question-contract correction checkpoint

This is an editorial and execution-evidence checkpoint, not a stable benchmark
release. The frozen preview and original 213-core review scope remain unchanged.

## Mathematical advance

LA206 previously asked for equality with a sum containing a compiler upper
budget. Its original answer proved only an inequality. The version-2 successor
first asks the reader to assess the old assertion: at one-by-one dimensions the
actual emit count is zero while the polynomial emit budget is one. It then asks
for the exact sum of actual stage costs, the componentwise upper bound and the
same-run deterministic factorization, with deficient-rank and empty cases intact.

The new question is not propositionally equivalent to the old equality request.
The distinct mathematical review explicitly records that change. The original
core ID, family, train split, graph/source scope and support-step count remain
reserved. Both contract hashes, full predecessor digest and exact successor
review are carried into the current registry and public reader export.

Three actual Lean support roots prove the general stage-cost identity and the
one-by-one actual/budget emit mismatch. The receipt checks their transitive
axioms, a real sorry control and a real wrong-target control. It binds the
inspected source, reproducible runner, candidate and exact existing proof inputs.
It is neither a native formal-view admission nor certification of all NL prose.

## Implementation boundary

Answer-only corrections still cannot alter a question contract. The first
explicit question correction requires version2, an exact rejected/ambiguous
predecessor, distinct content-bound review, full answer/rubric and separate
question provenance. Unknown assumption/split/graph overrides, stale hashes,
malformed payloads and colliding keys fail closed. Old-hash Lean companions are
not silently upgraded. Further successor chains and graph/assumption migrations
are not implemented by accepting another override.

Historical reader-review verdicts, including positive ones, remain source-bound
audit records and never count as approval of changed content. Independent
implementation review identified malformed-payload and typed-evidence gaps;
regressions were added for those cases rather than treating a review label as
sufficient structural validation.
